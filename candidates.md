# Candidates — The Word You'd Delete From the Dictionary

Competition: u-puan-the-word-you-d-delete-from-the-dictionary-9e774bc2 ($2, top 5 paid).
Gate: /output/entry.txt, UTF-8, 1–77 chars, one word, nothing else.

Judge brief, criteria in order:
1. Real word a reader recognises, to erase forever.
2. Deletion funny at first sight — joke lands the moment the word is gone, no caption.
3. Humour from the word itself — sound, double meanings, or the gap it leaves — not shock alone.
4. Fresh — a word the rest of the field would not think of first, still instantly understood.
5. Exact — one word, spelled correctly, nothing added.

## Manual ranking (brief criteria in order, then pairwise vs the strongest rival shapes)

All 14 pass criterion 1 (real, recognised) and 5 (one word, exact). The decision turns on 2–4.

- **queue vs moist**: moist is the field's obvious pick — its humour is disgust/shock, which the brief says does not count alone, and it loses criterion 4 badly (half the field will submit it; byte-identical copies share one rate). queue wins on 3 (the silent-letter absurdity IS the word) and 4.
- **queue vs taxes / Monday / deadline**: all three are wishful thinking — the joke is "I wish the thing went away", not the word. The brief's criterion 3 demands humour from the word itself. queue wins on 3 by a wide margin.
- **queue vs gullible**: gullible's joke is the famous dictionary prank, but it is the prank everyone knows, so the field will flood it (criterion 4 fails, duplicate risk), and the joke is about dictionaries in general, not the word itself. queue wins on 3 and 4.
- **queue vs literally**: literally's joke is overuse; it is also a likely field pick and needs the reader to think about usage habits (a beat). queue's joke is visible on the page instantly. queue wins on 2 and 4.
- **queue vs colonel** (closest rival): both are spelling-vs-sound jokes. colonel needs the reader to hear "kernel" in their head; queue's joke is visible in the letters themselves — four silent letters staring at you — so it lands faster in a text-only duel (criterion 2). queue also wins the tie-break: 5 bytes vs 7.
- **queue vs Wednesday**: Wednesday has two jokes (spelling + losing a weekday), but the weekday half is wishful thinking again, and its spelling joke (Wed-nes-day) is subtler than queue's four silent letters. queue wins on 2 and 3.
- **queue vs defenestration**: defenestration's gap joke is good (no word for the crime), but it needs a beat to land and many readers pause on the long word; queue lands at first sight. queue wins on 2.
- **queue vs kerfuffle / sesquipedalian / irregardless / phlegm**: kerfuffle and sesquipedalian are self-referential but the joke is mild; irregardless's joke is an in-joke for pedants; phlegm is shock/disgust again. None beats queue on criterion 2.

## Winner: **queue**

- Criterion 2: the joke is in the spelling, visible the instant you read it — a five-letter word with one sound.
- Criterion 3: the humour is the word itself (four silent letters doing nothing); deleting it removes a word that barely works.
- Criterion 4: fresh — the field will reach for moist, taxes, Monday, gullible first; queue is the clever spelling pick, still instantly understood by every reader.
- Criterion 5: one word, spelled correctly, 5 bytes (helps the exact-tie break: smaller text places first).
